About

I am a Ph.D. student in the Department of Brain and Cognitive Sciences at MIT, investigating the neural circuit mechanisms that underlie innate social behaviors.

Before starting my Ph.D., I worked as a research intern in the Dayu Lin Lab at the NYU Neuroscience Institute, where I contributed to research on dopamine's role in modulating aggression. This work was published in Nature in 2025.

I completed my B.S. in Biomedical Science at Shanghai Jiao Tong University (SJTU). My interests span from neural circuit dissection and in vivo electrophysiology to the development of novel tools — including genetically encoded sensors and viral vectors — for probing brain function at the systems level.

Research Interests

Neural Circuits of Innate Behavior

Dissecting the subcortical circuits — including the hypothalamus, lateral septum, and brainstem — that generate and modulate innate social behaviors such as aggression, mating, and defense.

Dopamine & Neural Plasticity

Understanding how dopaminergic signaling from the VTA modulates circuit dynamics in an experience-dependent manner, and how synaptic plasticity reshapes behavioral output over time.

Novel Tools for Systems Neuroscience

Developing and applying genetically encoded fluorescent sensors (e.g., GRAB sensors), viral vectors (AAV), and advanced imaging approaches to observe neural activity with high spatiotemporal resolution.

Publications

Nature

Experience-dependent dopamine modulation of male aggression

Dai, B., Zheng, B., Dai, X., Cui, X., Yin, L., Cai, J., Zhuo, Y., Tritsch, N.X., Zweifel, L.S., Li, Y., Lin, D.

2025 Nature 639, 430–437

Demonstrated that VTA dopaminergic cells bidirectionally modulate aggression in male mice in an experience-dependent manner through the dorsal lateral septum, revealing a sophisticated role of dopamine in the emergence of aggressive behavior.
